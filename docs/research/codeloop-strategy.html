AssemblyAI Voice Agent Hackathon · lablab.ai · strategy as of 25 Sep 2026

CodeLoop

A voice agent that works as the recorder at an in-hospital cardiac arrest. It listens to the whole team, logs every drug and shock, and runs the protocol clock. It also catches spoken orders that nobody confirmed back.

00:00 CPR started 00:24 shock 200 J · closed 00:41 epi 1 mg · unacknowledged 01:02 amio 300 → read back 150 02:00 rhythm check due

Verdict: drop VoiceCase and Guardian and build CodeLoop

Both of our ideas land in the two most crowded clusters in this hackathon. There is already a submission called Voice Case. About 35–40 of the ~160 entries use the same "LLM proposes, code decides, quote the evidence, read it back" pattern that Guardian relies on. Nobody has built anything for resuscitation. CodeLoop keeps the best parts of both ideas: Guardian's deterministic engine, VoiceCase's Truth Ledger and the audit receipt. It moves them into a setting where voice is the only interface that works, the harm is fatal, and the evidence is published.

Whitespace

  • Agents that listen to a live multi-person human team and track who promised what to whom. Most entries are one user talking to one bot. Saakshi, Second Chair and Bisik monitor two people, never a team of 3–6.
  • Time-critical clinical team work: codes, trauma activations, neonatal resuscitation. Zero entries.
  • Demos judges can run without us, with numbers measured against live AssemblyAI. Rare even among the top entries.
  • Real audio conditions (noise, overlap, several speakers). Almost every demo is one clean mic.

Credibility lesson from the review

Our agents found several entries whose claims don't match their code: HazVox claims the Voice Agent API but uses browser speech recognition, one deployed frontend calls localhost, one "threat score" is clamped to 89–99.6, and one demo link is only a GitHub repo. With 21 engineering judges, our README must state plainly what is real, what is simulated, and how we measured every number.

PART COur existing ideas

VoiceCase drop as primary

  • Collisions: a submission literally named Voice Case, plus VerbaTrace, EvidenTurn, ClaimVoice and LoudEnough. Outside the hackathon: NYAYA (public GitHub, English/Hindi/Hinglish intake, evidence checklist and complaint drafting on Claude) and Reinstara ($34 per AI appeal, 9 platforms).
  • Voice necessity is medium. A worker could fill a WhatsApp form; voice helps but isn't essential.
  • Outcome risk: in Washington, 80% of deactivations were overturned when drivers had union representation under a fair-reason standard. What wins is representation plus a legal forum, not a well-written letter. Karnataka's grievance rules are under court challenge.
  • Who pays: unions and NGOs, not workers. This is an impact play, not a business.

GuardianVoice (OR/ICU) drop

  • AegisOR (OR timeouts and counts), Tell, MediScribe and Veritas already occupy clinical voice safety here.
  • Passive "audit" of drug administration invites the regulatory question with no answer in 5 days.
  • Clinical ambient AI is a crowded incumbent market (Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Abridge, Ambience, Epic).
  • The one real gap it points at, verbal orders that aren't read back (45% of respondents to an ISMP survey read them back less than half the time), is the seed CodeLoop grows from.

Modified Guardian (industrial) drop standalone, keep the engine

  • The pain is real: lockout/tagout was OSHA's #4 most-cited standard in FY2025 (2,562 citations).
  • But every piece exists commercially (Vocollect, RealWear, Augmentir, Siemens, Brady/Master Lock e-LOTO, Enablon/Sphera permit-to-work). A 2026 Springer paper published "LLM proposes, deterministic checker disposes".
  • In this hackathon: HazVox, Voice Action Gate, READBACK, Tally, Patchline and Earshot. Judges will have seen the pattern six times.
  • The demo would be a simulated plant, and "who certifies this?" has no answer.

What we keep. Guardian's deterministic engine becomes the ACLS protocol and closed-loop state machine. VoiceCase's Truth Ledger (VERIFIED / SPOKEN / CONFLICTING / UNKNOWN) becomes the status of each event (CLOSED / HEARD / CONFLICT / UNCONFIRMED). The Safety Receipt becomes the Code Record, where every line links to a timestamp, a speaker and the exact words heard. The team's thinking carries over; only the domain changes.

Why the major scores

  • CodeLoop: voice necessity 10. During a code, hands are compressing, bagging or pushing drugs, and eyes are on the patient and monitor. Existing recorder apps fail because they need taps. The spoken exchange is the workflow.
  • CodeLoop: AssemblyAI fit 10. It exercises streaming diarization, Medical Mode, keyterms swapped mid-session, word confidence, far-field Voice Focus, Hindi/English code-switching, and the Voice Agent API with semantic barge-in. Each is needed for a specific failure mode, not bolted on.
  • CodeLoop: feasibility 7, not 9. Multi-speaker far-field audio is the real risk. We de-risk on day 1 and have a fallback of one phone mic per role, one stream per channel.
  • CodeLoop: production 6. Live alerting is probably regulated software. The documentation and simulation-debrief modes are not, so there is a real path to market.
  • Faithful: originality 9 but feasibility 5 and safety 6. Checking meaning across languages causes false flags on legitimate paraphrase. Judges must understand two languages to feel the demo, and a wrong flag can undermine an interpreter unfairly.
  • Verbal Order Guard: whitespace 5. It is the best technical showcase of word confidence, but judges will read it as another read-back gate. CodeLoop contains it: the dose read-back mismatch is one of CodeLoop's catches.
  • Guardian and VoiceCase: originality 3–4. Direct name and pattern collisions in this hackathon.

Why CodeLoop beats the rest

  1. It is the only entry in its category. Originality is a quarter of the score, and most of the field loses it.
  2. Voice is the problem, not the interface. Failure happens in human-to-human speech: 387 verbal orders in 89 filmed trauma resuscitations, and only 26% were closed-loop.
  3. It turns the crowded pattern into a feature. Judges have seen "code decides" 35 times on bank transfers. Here, code holding the ACLS clock and the orders ledger is obviously right.
  4. It is the most AssemblyAI-heavy idea we found, and every feature is load-bearing.
  5. The demo is universally legible. Everyone knows "code blue" from TV. An amber card saying "epinephrine 1 mg: nobody confirmed" needs no explanation.

We can't promise a "definite #1". There are 5 equal prizes, 21 judges and no published weights, and at least six competitors have months-grade polish. What we can say from the evidence is that CodeLoop gives us the best odds of any idea we examined. The remaining risk is execution, which Part L addresses.

PART GHackathon MVP scope

MUST proves the concept

  • Room audio → U3.5 Pro streaming with speaker_labels, domain=medical-v1, ACLS keyterms_prompt, detailed prompt, voice_focus=far-field.
  • Role call at code start ("I'm leading", "Priya, meds") maps speaker labels to roles. Manual reassignment by tap.
  • Event extraction: a fast grammar path plus LLM Gateway json_schema. Every event must quote the exact transcript span, or it is rejected.
  • Deterministic ACLS engine: code clock, 2-minute CPR cycles, epi 3–5 min window, shock count, amiodarone 300 → 150 sequence, rhythm path.
  • Closed-loop ledger: ORDERED → ACKNOWLEDGED → DONE. UNACKNOWLEDGED after 10 s. CONFLICT on dose or energy mismatch. UNCONFIRMED when dose-word confidence is below 0.6.
  • Voice Agent API voice: short prompts via reply.create, and questions ("CodeLoop, last epi?") answered only through tools. Instant barge-in.
  • Live UI: clock, timers, open-loops board, event timeline with quote, speaker and confidence.
  • Code Record export (JSON + printable HTML), with unresolved items flagged for sign-off.
  • Replay mode on the hosted demo: streams a recorded mock code through the real pipeline.
  • Public repo with a README section on what's real and what's simulated. 5-minute video, PDF deck.

SHOULD lifts the score

  • Evaluation harness: 6–8 scripted mock codes with gold event logs. Event recall and precision, dose accuracy, timestamp error, loop-detection accuracy.
  • A/B scoreboard: the same audio with and without Medical Mode, keyterms and prompt. Drug and dose entity error rates side by side.
  • Hindi/English code-switching ("epi ek milligram de do") with a per-turn language_code chip.
  • Rhythm consistency flag: "charge" ordered while the last recorded rhythm is asystole or PEA → "Check rhythm: last recorded asystole."
  • Echo guard so CodeLoop never logs its own voice.
  • Debrief view: timeline chart, time-to-first-epi, loop closure rate, prompts issued.
  • Latency panel: speech-to-event ms and agent time_to_first_audio_ms.

NICE if time remains

  • Fallback mode: one phone per role as a mic (one stream per channel, perfect attribution).
  • FHIR Bundle export (Observation / MedicationAdministration / Procedure).
  • GWTG-R / Utstein-style form pre-fill.
  • Pediatric weight-based dose check ("epi 0.01 mg/kg").
  • Session-end pipeline: fetch the Voice Agent timeline and add it to the record.
  • A simple sim-instructor dashboard comparing runs.

DON'T BUILD

  • Any drug recommendation beyond protocol timing. We record and remind; we never prescribe.
  • Defibrillator or monitor integration (state it as roadmap).
  • Real EHR connection, auth, multi-tenant admin, billing.
  • Compression-rate detection from audio (LifeLine's feature, and a rabbit hole).
  • Mobile native app, custom voice, fancy 3D visuals.
  • Telephony. A code isn't a phone call.

PART HFull production product

User experience

  • A tablet mounted on every crash cart, plus an optional ceiling mic array in ICU and ED bays.
  • Starts when someone says "code blue" or presses one large button. Stops at ROSC or termination.
  • The screen is readable from 3 m: the clock, what's due, open loops.
  • Voice prompts are short and rare, and humans can silence them ("CodeLoop, quiet").

Onboarding

  • A hospital configures its formulary, protocol version (AHA 2025 guidelines / ERC / pediatric), prompt policy and languages.
  • Teams train on mock codes first, the sim tier.
  • Opt-in voice enrollment of regular code-team members improves attribution.

Core workflow

  1. Activation, then role call.
  2. Live capture and the ACLS clock.
  3. Loop monitoring and prompts.
  4. ROSC or termination.
  5. Nurse review queue for UNCONFIRMED and CONFLICT items.
  6. Sign-off.
  7. EHR flowsheet and registry export.
  8. Debrief within minutes.

Voice experience

  • Three prompt levels per hospital policy: silent (screen only), timers only, timers plus loop nudges.
  • Semantic barge-in: any human speech cuts the agent off.
  • Direct questions answered only from the ledger.
  • Configurable voice and volume.

Web / mobile

  • Crash-cart PWA in kiosk mode.
  • Web console for QA, educators and admins.
  • Read-only live view for a remote intensivist or tele-ICU.

Backend

  • Python async orchestrator per active code (holds both AssemblyAI sockets, the engine and the extractor).
  • An event bus for state changes and a Postgres system of record.
  • Object storage for encrypted audio.
  • A worker queue for post-code jobs.

AI architecture

  • AssemblyAI U3.5 Pro streaming (ears), Voice Agent API (voice), LLM Gateway (extraction, debrief summary).
  • The LLM classifies and extracts; the ACLS engine and ledger decide.
  • Offline evaluation sets per hospital and regression tests on every model or prompt change.

Agent architecture

  • The voice agent sees tools only (get_code_state, get_events, confirm_event, correct_event, mute_prompts, set_role).
  • Its system prompt forbids stating any time, dose or rhythm not returned by a tool.
  • Progressive tool reveal: correction tools unlock only after the code ends, or for the recorder role.

Database

  • Postgres tables: codes, participants, utterances (words, confidence, speaker), events, loops, prompts, corrections, signoffs, audit_log (append-only, hash-chained).
  • Audio in encrypted object storage.

Auth & permissions

  • Hospital SSO (SAML/OIDC).
  • Device certificates for carts.
  • Roles: clinician (live), recorder-nurse (review and sign), QA/educator (read, de-identified), admin (config).
  • Break-glass access is logged.

Audit logging

  • Every event links to utterance IDs, word offsets and the audio time range.
  • Every correction keeps the original and records who changed it and when.
  • Every prompt spoken is logged with its trigger rule.
  • Hash-chained so the record is tamper-evident.

Security & privacy

  • TLS everywhere, KMS-encrypted storage, a BAA with AssemblyAI and the LLM provider (US), and regional processing (AssemblyAI EU/US endpoints).
  • India: DPDP Act 2023 consent and notice.
  • PII redaction on transcripts leaving the clinical record.

Data retention

  • Audio kept 30 days by default (policy-configurable), then deleted. The signed record follows the medical-record retention period.
  • De-identified timelines are kept for QA and research under the hospital's own agreement.

Human-in-the-loop

  • No entry reaches the EHR without nurse sign-off.
  • CONFLICT and UNCONFIRMED items block sign-off until resolved.
  • The team leader can silence or override any prompt.
  • Every prompt is framed as a reminder, never an instruction.

Failure handling

The agent gets mic audio only after the Ears stream hears the wake word "CodeLoop" (a ~6 s window), and during its own speech so that barge-in works. Protocol prompts come from the engine via reply.create with "Say exactly: …" instructions from the fixed phrase list. Closed-loop state machine

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> ORDERED: order heard (quote verified)
  ORDERED --> ACKNOWLEDGED: matching read-back
  ORDERED --> UNACKNOWLEDGED: no ack in 10 s
  UNACKNOWLEDGED --> ACKNOWLEDGED: late ack
  ORDERED --> CONFLICT: read-back value differs
  ACKNOWLEDGED --> CONFLICT: done value differs
  CONFLICT --> ACKNOWLEDGED: leader restates value
  ACKNOWLEDGED --> DONE: "is in" or "delivered"
  ORDERED --> CANCELLED: explicit cancel
  ACKNOWLEDGED --> CANCELLED: explicit cancel
  DONE --> [*]
  CANCELLED --> [*]

Any value built on a dose word below 0.6 confidence carries an UNCONFIRMED flag through every state until a human resolves it. DONE drug events feed the ACLS engine: epi resets the 3–5 minute window, and amiodarone advances the 300 → 150 sequence. Shocks increment the count and check against the last rhythm.

Error handling

  • AssemblyAI stream errors: 3005 or 1006 → reconnect with backoff and a new temp token, keep buffering audio. Show a banner. 3007 (chunk size) is a client bug and is covered by a test.
  • Voice Agent: resume within 30 s via session.resume. After that, open a new session. Prompts go to screen-only in the meantime.
  • Gateway timeout above 2.5 s: keep the grammar result as HEARD and retry the LLM asynchronously.
  • Echo: drop Ears turns that overlap agent playback and fuzzy-match (≥0.8) the agent's text.
  • Billing safety: always send Terminate and session.end. Hard-cap each demo session at 15 minutes.

PART K20 hard judge questions

1. Why voice? Why not a tap app like Redivus?

During a code, the hands that could tap are doing compressions, airway or drugs. The recorder role exists because someone has to stop caring for the patient to write. Tap apps record what someone remembered to enter. CodeLoop records what was actually said, including orders nobody acknowledged, which a tap app can't see at all.

2. Why AssemblyAI specifically?

We need far-field, multi-speaker streaming with medical vocabulary, word-level confidence and Hindi/English code-switching, plus an agent that stops talking the instant a human speaks. U3.5 Pro streaming (diarization, Medical Mode, keyterms, Voice Focus) and the Voice Agent API's semantic barge-in cover that in one stack. We show the A/B: the same audio without Medical Mode and keyterms, and the drug-name error difference.

3. Isn't this just ChatGPT with a microphone?

No. The LLM only turns sentences into candidate events, and each must quote the transcript verbatim. The clock, timers, loop states, protocol checks and every number spoken come from deterministic, unit-tested code. The voice agent can only read state through tools. Remove the LLM and the engine still runs on the grammar path.

4. Who actually needs this?

Hospital code-blue and rapid-response teams, and the resuscitation committees and educators who must review every code. First users are simulation educators running mock codes and ACLS courses: no patient data, no regulatory barrier, and they need timelines for debriefing.

5. Why don't existing products solve it?

Defibrillator review tools see shocks and compressions but not drugs or conversation. Tap apps need hands. Ambient scribes are built for one-on-one visits, not six people talking at once under time pressure. Nobody tracks closed loops live.

6. What happens when the AI is wrong?

Wrong entries can't silently become facts. Low-confidence dose words become UNCONFIRMED. Disagreements become CONFLICT. Nothing is exported without nurse sign-off. Prompts are reminders, and the leader can silence them. In our evaluation we report the error rates rather than hiding them.

7. How do you prevent hallucinations?

Three gates. (1) The extraction quote must match the transcript verbatim. (2) Values must be in the formulary and within bounds. (3) The agent may only speak numbers returned by tools, and its prompts come from a fixed phrase list. We log every dropped extraction.

8. What about ambiguity, like two drugs ordered at once or overlapping speech?

Each order opens its own loop, and matching uses drug, dose and a time window, not just the speaker. Overlap that diarization labels PENDING/UNKNOWN gets attributed as "unknown speaker" and flagged. We would rather show uncertainty than guess.

9. A talking machine in a code sounds distracting. Why would teams trust it?

By default it speaks only for the 2-minute cycle and broken loops, in a few words, and stops the moment anyone talks. Hospitals choose silent, timers-only or full. Trust comes from the evidence trail: every line on screen shows the exact words it came from.

10. Who pays, and how much?

First, hospital simulation and education budgets, as a per-site subscription. Later, the clinical documentation tier per bed, justified by recorder time saved, registry abstraction time, and quality metrics like time to epinephrine. Prices are hypotheses we'll test with educators. Our AssemblyAI cost is about $2.70 per 30-minute code at list price.

11. How big is the market?

About 290k adult in-hospital cardiac arrests a year in the US alone. Every acute hospital runs codes and mock codes. The same engine extends to trauma activations, pediatric and neonatal resuscitation, and EMS. We won't quote a TAM we can't source; the sourced numbers are in our deck appendix.

12. What's defensible?

The protocol engine and loop ledger are simple to copy. What compounds is (a) labelled resuscitation audio and evaluation sets from sim centres, (b) per-hospital formulary and protocol configurations, (c) EHR and registry integrations, and (d) the regulatory file for the clinical tier.

13. What if Epic or an ambient-scribe company copies it?

They might, eventually. Their products are built for one-on-one visits. Codes are a niche with different audio, timing and team dynamics. Our wedge is simulation training, where they don't sell. Integration partner is a likely outcome too.

14. Can this actually be deployed? Isn't it a medical device?

The simulation and debrief tier can be deployed now; it isn't used on patients. Documentation with nurse sign-off is the next step. Live time-critical alerting is likely regulated as SaMD, so we'd pursue an FDA pre-submission and the CDSCO route. We say this upfront rather than hiding it.

15. What did you build versus simulate?

Real: live AssemblyAI streaming, diarization, Medical Mode, keyterms, the Voice Agent API, LLM Gateway extraction, the ACLS engine, the loop ledger, the record export and the evaluation. Simulated: the patient and monitor (actors and a manikin), and there's no EHR or defibrillator integration. The README has this table.

16. Which part of AssemblyAI is essential? What breaks without it?

Without streaming diarization we can't tell orders from acknowledgments. Without word confidence a misheard "fifteen" becomes a fact. Without Medical Mode and keyterms, drug names degrade (we show the numbers). Without semantic barge-in the agent talks over the team leader.

17. How accurate is it in a real, loud code?

We don't know yet and won't claim to. We measured on N scripted mock codes with alarm and compression noise, and report recall, precision and dose accuracy. Real-world validation is the first milestone of a sim-centre pilot.

18. What about privacy of recording clinicians and patients?

In simulation there's no patient data. Clinically: hospital policy and consent, encrypted storage, a BAA with vendors, audio deleted after 30 days by default, and redaction of identifiers in exports. India: DPDP Act consent and notice.

19. Why would the team leader accept being "audited" by a machine?

The framing is support, not surveillance. It removes the recorder burden and produces the debrief the committee requires anyway. Mock codes are already filmed and debriefed; CodeLoop makes that faster and more objective.

20. Why did a team from India build a US-guideline product?

ACLS is international (AHA and ERC), and Indian hospitals run code-blue teams under NABH. Our Hinglish support reflects how Indian teams actually talk. We'd pilot with Indian teaching hospitals and simulation centres, where we can reach educators directly.
